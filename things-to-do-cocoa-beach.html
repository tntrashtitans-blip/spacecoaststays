Cocoa Beach Guide / Things To Do

Cocoa Beach is a small beach city — about 11,000 residents, a single main road, and the Atlantic Ocean on one side and the Indian River Lagoon on the other. What makes it different from most Florida beach towns is what is directly to the north: Kennedy Space Center, the most active rocket launch site in the world. That combination — real surf culture, practical beach living, and front-row seats to space launches — is what most visitors are not expecting when they arrive.

This guide covers the full range of activities: beach basics, water sports, rocket launches, KSC, shopping, dining, nightlife, and the best day trips. For a venue-by-venue directory with photos and links, see the Cocoa Beach Guide. For planning your stay timing around launches or seasons, see the best time to visit Cocoa Beach.

Beaches and swimming

Cocoa Beach's public beach access runs the length of N Atlantic Ave (A1A). The best-equipped spots are Sidney Fischer Park and Lori Wilson Park, both of which have parking, restrooms, picnic areas, and seasonal lifeguards. Lori Wilson is quieter and better for families. Sidney Fischer is closer to the main commercial strip.

The beach itself is wide, with tan sand and moderate wave action most days. Swells run 2–4 feet on average — enough to body surf or boogie board without committing to a surf lesson. Rip currents are real: watch the flag system posted at each access point. Red flag means no swimming; yellow means use caution.

Jetty Park Beach at Port Canaveral, 6 miles north, is a strong alternative for families — there's a fishing pier, a campground, and you can watch cruise ships and Navy vessels pass within a few hundred feet of shore. It's also one of the best free spots to watch a rocket launch if your timing lines up.

Best beach tip

The best beach hours are 7–10am before the onshore breeze picks up and the sand fills in. Pack in early, claim your spot, and plan lunch in town before the midday crowd arrives.

Surf lessons, kayaking, and water sports

Cocoa Beach is the birthplace of East Coast surfing — Kelly Slater grew up here, and Ron Jon Surf Shop opened on this beach in 1959. The culture is still genuine. Waves are not massive, but the breaks are consistent enough to make learning realistic.

Cocoa Beach Surf School offers beginner lessons for all ages, group sessions for families, and more advanced instruction for guests who already know how to stand up. Lessons run on the beach near the main access points. First-timers consistently get up on a board. If surfing is on the list, this is the cleanest way to make it happen without wasting half the day figuring it out solo.

On the Banana River side of the barrier island, the Thousand Islands Conservation Area is a completely different experience. Paddling through mangrove tunnels in glassy flat water, you'll see manatees, roseate spoonbills, ospreys, and occasionally dolphins. Several outfitters offer guided kayak and paddleboard tours. It's calm enough for kids and genuinely beautiful in a way that surprises people who came only for the ocean.

Fishing charters depart from Port Canaveral year-round. The port is one of the more productive offshore fishing hubs on the East Coast, with access to deep-water bottom fishing, trolling for mahi and wahoo, and inshore snook and redfish trips in the lagoon. Half-day boats leave early morning and are a good option for groups or guests who want to feel like they did something local and active.

Dolphin and wildlife boat tours loop through the port waterway and lagoon. They're reliable — the Indian River Lagoon is one of the most biodiverse estuaries in North America — and a low-commitment option for guests who want water time without the surf or paddle commitment.

Rocket launches and Kennedy Space Center

This is what makes Cocoa Beach genuinely different from every other Florida beach town.

SpaceX launches from Cape Canaveral at a pace that would have been unimaginable a decade ago — sometimes multiple times per week. When a Falcon 9 goes up at night, you can watch it from the beach, the balcony, or the Cocoa Beach Pier. The sky lights up, the ground shakes a few seconds later, and then the sonic boom arrives 6–8 minutes after liftoff if it's an RTLS booster landing at the Cape. It's not a planned attraction. It's just something that happens here.

The rocket launch guide on this site has a live countdown to the next scheduled launch, upcoming missions, best free viewing spots, and how to track the SpaceX drone ship when it returns through Port Canaveral after a landing at sea.

Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex is 12 miles north of Cocoa Beach — about a 20-minute drive. It's a legitimate full-day experience: the Space Shuttle Atlantis exhibit, a Saturn V rocket you can stand under, astronaut encounters, IMAX films, and bus tours out to the launch pads. If a launch is scheduled during your stay, KSC sells tickets to the Apollo/Saturn V Center viewing area at Banana Creek — the closest public land to the pads. It sells out. Book early.

The Pier, Ron Jon, and the main strip

The Cocoa Beach Pier extends 800 feet over the Atlantic at the end of Meade Ave, about 1 mile south of Sandcastles. It has multiple bars, a restaurant, and a surf shop, and it hosts live music on weekends. It's easy to walk to, easy to spend an hour at, and the views back toward the beach are good. During launches, it becomes a natural gathering point because the elevated deck gives an unobstructed sightline north.

Ron Jon Surf Shop is open 24 hours, 365 days a year — a claim almost no retail store anywhere can make. It's 50,000 square feet of surf gear, beach apparel, and Cocoa Beach souvenirs. Even guests who are not buying anything usually end up walking through. It is genuinely part of the Cocoa Beach experience in a way that is hard to explain and easy to understand once you're standing in there at midnight.

The main strip along N Atlantic Ave has a mix of chain restaurants, local spots, beach bars, and surf shops. It's compact and walkable if you're staying near the middle of Cocoa Beach. Guests who want a quieter evening often drive 6 miles north to the Port Canaveral waterfront: Grills Seafood Deck & Tiki Bar and Fishlips Waterfront Bar & Grill are two of the better waterfront dinner options, with views of the port channel and occasional SpaceX ship activity.

Day trips from Cocoa Beach

Canaveral National Seashore, 12 miles north, is 24 miles of undeveloped Atlantic beach with sea turtle nesting, birding, and almost none of the commercial infrastructure you'll find in central Cocoa Beach. It's one of the longest undeveloped barrier island beaches on the East Coast. Bring food, water, and sunscreen — there's almost nothing out there, which is the point. Access is from New Smyrna Beach to the north (Apollo Beach) or from SR-528 to the south (Playalinda Beach).

Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge wraps around Kennedy Space Center and is adjacent to the launch pads. The Black Point Wildlife Drive is a 7-mile loop through wetlands where alligators, manatees, herons, roseate spoonbills, and American bald eagles are regularly spotted. The drive takes about 90 minutes. It's free and genuinely wild in a way that surprises people who aren't expecting it this close to a launch facility.

Brevard Zoo in Melbourne, 25 miles south, is one of the better regional zoos in Florida — kayak-through animal habitats, giraffe feeding, and a solid full-day family option when beach days need a break. Downtown Melbourne, adjacent to the zoo, has a walkable arts and restaurant district worth a dinner trip.

Cocoa Village, 20 minutes west on the Indian River, is the historic downtown of Cocoa — different energy from the beach, with independent shops, a Saturday farmers market, and good brunch spots. Orlando is 50 miles west, about an hour by car — close enough for a theme park day trip without committing to a hotel in either direction.

Restaurants and dining

Cocoa Beach has a solid restaurant range for a town of its size. Breakfast and brunch are particularly strong: Simply Delicious Cafe & Bakery is the longtime local favorite for generous portions and fresh baked goods. Whisk & Grind and The Surfinista cover the coffee and smoothie bowl side of the morning.

For dinner, the options range from casual to sit-down: 4th Street Fillin Station for drinks and a lively room, Gregory's on the Beach for a classic seafood dinner, Florida's Fresh Grill for a polished local meal, and Brano's Italian Grill for pasta and wine when the group wants something easy. Long Doggers is the reliable catch-all for lunch — sandwiches, bowls, seafood, and a relaxed pace.

At Port Canaveral, Grills Seafood Deck & Tiki Bar is the best waterfront dinner option in the area — outdoor seating, live music on weekends, fresh fish, and views of the port channel where the cruise ships come in.

Common Questions

What are the top things to do in Cocoa Beach?

Swimming and beach time, surf lessons at Cocoa Beach Surf School, watching a rocket launch from the shoreline, visiting Kennedy Space Center, kayaking the Thousand Islands, fishing out of Port Canaveral, and walking the Cocoa Beach Pier. Most visitors also make a stop at Ron Jon Surf Shop regardless of whether they're buying anything.

Can you watch a rocket launch from Cocoa Beach?

Yes — it's one of the best free viewing areas for SpaceX and NASA launches. The beach shoreline faces north toward the Kennedy Space Center launch pads, about 12 miles away. Jetty Park at Port Canaveral (6 miles north) is the closest free public land to the pads. Oceanfront units at Sandcastles Condominiums have a direct northwest sightline to LC-39A and SLC-40. The launch guide has the current countdown and upcoming schedule.

Is Cocoa Beach good for families with kids?

Yes. Lifeguard beaches at Sidney Fischer and Lori Wilson Parks, beginner surf lessons, the Brevard Zoo 25 miles south, Kennedy Space Center, and the Cocoa Beach Pier are all family-friendly options. Condo-style rentals at Sandcastles give families kitchen space, laundry, and room to spread out — more practical than hotel rooms for most family trips.

How far is Kennedy Space Center from Cocoa Beach?

The Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex is about 12 miles north — a 20-minute drive via SR-528. Plan a full day. The Atlantis exhibit alone takes 2–3 hours, and the bus tour to the launch area adds another 2 hours. If a launch is scheduled, KSC sells premium viewing tickets that sell out in advance.
